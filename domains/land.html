Every warfighter knows the feeling: you advance past cell coverage, the satellite link cuts out, and suddenly the most critical communication of your life is running on a radio that hasn't fundamentally changed since the Cold War.

One Island Tech, T3E97 Protocol

The Problem

Infrastructure is
a vulnerability, not a feature.

Modern communication systems were designed around the assumption that infrastructure exists: cell towers, satellite uplinks, network operations centers monitoring the connection. That assumption fails in exactly the situations where communication matters most: active combat, natural disasters, remote terrain, denied environments.

When the tower goes down, the uplink gets jammed, or the team advances beyond coverage range, traditional systems don't degrade gracefully. They stop. T3E97 removes the dependency entirely.

How T3E97 Works

Communication that survives
because it never depended on the network.

T3E97 converts voice into encrypted intent capsules before any transmission occurs. No audio signal ever enters any medium at any point. The capsule travels as a discrete encrypted package over whatever channel is available: LoRa radio, mesh relay, USB, or any other carrier. The system requires no licensed spectrum, no base station, and no carrier agreement.

The communication travels as a discrete, encrypted package, not a streaming audio call. A stream breaks when the link drops. A discrete package finds a way through. The same voice arrives at the destination regardless of what the link does in between.

Where This Gets Deployed

Three very different environments.
One system that operates in all of them.

01
Special Operations, Denied Environments
Teams advancing through terrain where cell networks don't exist and satellite links are either jammed or operationally inadvisable. T3E97 doesn't ask for permission from any infrastructure. It creates its own communication path between the endpoints that matter, using whatever channel is available. No NOC. No uplink. No single point of failure to disable.
02
Disaster Response, When the Grid Is Gone
Earthquakes, hurricanes, catastrophic floods: the infrastructure that first responders depend on is often the first thing to fail. Cell towers topple. Satellite bandwidth gets saturated by thousands of people simultaneously. T3E97 deploys in the time it takes to walk from a staging area to the impact zone. The hardware is small enough that every team member carries a node. Communication comes up before anything else does.
03
Remote Industrial Operations
Mining operations, offshore construction, wildland fire lines, pipeline inspection in remote corridors: environments where workers operate far outside cellular coverage and satellite voice is either too expensive, too slow, or simply unavailable when the sky is blocked. T3E97 covers the kind of terrain that nothing else does, at latency that makes voice communication feel like a real conversation rather than a satellite delay exercise.
